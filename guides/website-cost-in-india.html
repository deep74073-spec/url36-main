Website Development Cost in India: What Affects the Price?

Website development costs in India vary because every project has a different scope. A small business website with a few information pages needs different work from an online store, booking system or custom web application. The best way to understand a quotation is to check exactly what the developer will design, build, deliver and support.

1. Start with the type of website you need

The website's purpose is one of the first things that affects the work involved.

  • Informational website: Shares business details, services, location and contact information.
  • Portfolio website: Presents work samples, skills, case studies or professional information.
  • Business website: May include service pages, enquiry forms, testimonials and location information.
  • Product catalogue: Displays products and specifications. A catalogue does not necessarily need online payments.
  • E-commerce website: May need product management, cart, checkout, payment integration, order notifications and delivery settings.
  • Custom web application: Can include user accounts, dashboards, workflows or other specialised functions.

Do not select a more complicated website type unless its features support a real business requirement.

2. Number of pages and design requirements

A project with several unique page layouts generally requires more planning and development than a small site using a consistent layout. The amount of content, languages, forms and special sections can also affect the work.

Ask whether the quotation includes mobile-friendly layouts, accessibility considerations, browser testing and design revisions. Clarify how many revision rounds are included and what counts as a new feature rather than a correction.

3. Features and third-party integrations

Features should be listed individually in the project scope. Examples include:

  • Contact and enquiry forms
  • WhatsApp or click-to-call links
  • Appointment or booking systems
  • Product catalogues and search
  • Payment gateways and checkout
  • Customer accounts or dashboards
  • Maps, email services or other external integrations

Some integrations may require separate subscriptions or transaction fees. Ask who will create and manage the accounts, who pays those charges and whether the integration will be tested before launch.

4. Content, images and basic SEO

Website content is part of the project, not just decoration. Confirm who will write the text, provide business information, prepare product details and supply photographs. Original graphics, professional photography, translation and copywriting may be separate services.

Basic SEO setup may include descriptive page titles, meta descriptions, logical headings, readable URLs, image alternative text and sitemap preparation. This is different from ongoing SEO services, content marketing or link building. No developer can guarantee a particular search ranking simply by completing basic SEO setup.

5. Domain, hosting and other recurring expenses

A domain name and web hosting are usually separate from the initial development work. Domain registration is renewed periodically, while hosting is commonly billed monthly or annually. Email services, premium themes, plugins and other tools may also have recurring charges.

Check the renewal price rather than relying only on an introductory offer. Confirm whether the domain and hosting accounts will be registered in your name and whether you will have direct access to them.

For more detail, read the Domain Name and Web Hosting guide.

6. Maintenance, updates and support

A website may need software updates, backups, security checks, content changes and troubleshooting after launch. Ask whether these tasks are included in the project or covered by a separate maintenance arrangement.

Clarify the support period, response process, tasks covered and charges for work outside the agreement. Also ask how you can request changes when your business or services grow.

7. Example: planning a five-page business website

Imagine a local repair service that wants a simple website. This is an illustrative example, not a fixed package or a quote for a real business.

  1. Home: A short introduction, key services and a clear enquiry option.
  2. About: Genuine business background and relevant experience.
  3. Services: Accurate descriptions of the services offered.
  4. Service area: Locations served, where relevant.
  5. Contact: Phone, email, WhatsApp link or an enquiry form.

The business should decide whether it needs only these pages or additional features such as appointment booking, multiple languages or online payments. Each additional requirement should be described separately before a developer prepares the quotation.

8. Prepare a website budget worksheet

Before requesting quotations, make a list of the work you need. Ask the developer to mark each item as included, optional or excluded.

  • Domain registration and renewal
  • Hosting plan and renewal
  • Number of pages and unique layouts
  • Responsive design and development
  • Content writing and image preparation
  • Forms, WhatsApp links or booking features
  • Payment or other third-party integrations
  • Basic SEO setup and sitemap
  • Testing, launch and domain connection
  • Backups, maintenance and technical support

This worksheet helps you identify missing items and compare quotations with similar requirements.

9. Separate one-time and recurring costs

Possible one-time project costs

  • Planning and initial design
  • Page development and agreed custom features
  • Initial content entry and image preparation
  • Testing and launch assistance

Possible recurring costs

  • Domain renewal and hosting subscription
  • Business email or other online services
  • Paid themes, plugins or software subscriptions
  • Maintenance, backups and ongoing support

Ask for these costs in writing. Also confirm whether applicable taxes, payment processing charges or third-party fees are included or billed separately.

10. Questions to ask before accepting a quotation

Request a written scope of work and check these points before making a decision:

  1. How many pages, layouts and features are included?
  2. Will the website work on mobile phones, tablets and desktop screens?
  3. Who provides the text, photographs and other content?
  4. How many design revisions are included?
  5. Who owns and controls the domain, hosting account and website files?
  6. Are forms, HTTPS setup, basic SEO and sitemap preparation included?
  7. What are the renewal, maintenance and support charges?
  8. What is the estimated delivery timeline and payment schedule?
  9. How will additional pages or features be priced?

Compare quotations by scope, deliverables, ownership and support—not just by the final amount. Keep a copy of the agreement and make sure you can access the important accounts and files.

11. Common website budget mistakes

  • Choosing only the lowest quote: Check what is excluded and whether the scope is comparable.
  • Ignoring renewal charges: Introductory prices may differ from later renewal prices.
  • Adding unnecessary features: Start with the functions visitors actually need.
  • Overlooking content: Decide who will prepare accurate text, images and product information.
  • Not checking ownership: Confirm access to the domain, hosting and website files.
  • Assuming SEO guarantees rankings: Basic setup cannot guarantee search traffic or a particular position.
  • Forgetting maintenance: Plan who will handle updates, backups and technical support.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there one fixed price for every website in India?

No. The cost depends on the website type, number of pages, design, features, content, integrations and support requirements. Ask for a quotation based on your actual scope.

Are domain and hosting included in website development?

They may be included in some quotations and separate in others. Ask who pays for renewals and whether the accounts will be registered in your name.

What should a website development quotation include?

It should describe the pages, features, design work, content responsibilities, revisions, timeline, payment terms, ownership, exclusions and recurring costs.

Can I start with a small website and add features later?

Yes, if the initial website is planned with future needs in mind. Start with essential pages and features, then add functions when there is a clear requirement.

Does basic SEO guarantee that my website will rank on Google?

No. Basic SEO helps search engines understand website pages, but rankings depend on many factors and cannot be guaranteed by a development package.
