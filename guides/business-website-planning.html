How to Plan a Business Website

A useful business website helps visitors understand what you offer, decide whether your business can meet their needs and contact you easily. Planning the website before design begins can save time and help you focus on information that matters to customers.

1. Decide what the website should achieve

Start by identifying the main purpose of the website. You may want visitors to call your business, request a quotation, book an appointment, visit your shop or learn about your services. Choose one main action and make it easy to find.

Next, think about your likely visitors. What do they need to know before contacting you? For example, they may want to understand your prices, service area, working hours, delivery options or the process for placing an order. Use these questions to guide the website content.

2. Choose pages that serve a clear purpose

A small business website can often begin with four main pages:

  • Home: Explain what the business offers, who it serves and how visitors can take the next step.
  • About: Share accurate information about the business, its background and the people behind it.
  • Services or Products: Describe each important service or product, including relevant details, limitations and pricing information where available.
  • Contact: Provide working contact methods, service area and location details when relevant.

Add other pages, such as frequently asked questions, a portfolio, booking information or a catalogue, when they answer a genuine visitor need. Every page should have a clear purpose rather than existing only to increase the page count.

3. Prepare specific and accurate content

Write in plain language. Explain what the business does, who it helps and what a customer should expect. Avoid vague claims such as being the best or guaranteeing results unless you can properly support them.

Use original photographs of your business, products or work where possible. Make sure you have permission to use images and that captions describe them accurately. Do not publish invented testimonials, client logos, qualifications or project results.

Before writing, collect the correct business name, contact details, service descriptions, prices or quotation process, service locations and frequently asked customer questions. This reduces errors and makes the website more useful.

4. Example: planning a website for an AC repair business

Imagine a local AC repair business wants customers to request a service visit. Its homepage could explain the repair services offered, the areas covered and how to request an appointment.

A services page could describe AC inspection, routine servicing and repair work, but only if the business actually provides those services. The contact page could show its verified phone number, working hours and service area. A short FAQ could answer practical questions about appointment requests and information customers should provide.

This example is a planning illustration, not a claim about an actual URL36 client or completed project. The real website should always reflect the business's confirmed services and details.

5. Make contact details and forms useful

Show a correct phone number or email address and make the main contact option easy to find. If the business serves a particular area, describe that area clearly. If customers visit a physical location, check the address and opening hours before publishing them.

If you use a contact form, test it on both a phone and a computer. Check that required fields are clear, submissions reach the correct inbox and visitors receive an understandable confirmation. Collect only the information needed to respond to the enquiry, and explain how submitted information is handled in the site's privacy information.

6. Check the mobile experience

Many people browse business websites on phones. Check that paragraphs are readable without zooming, navigation is easy to use, buttons have enough space around them and important contact details are visible.

Open the website on an actual mobile device if possible. Test important pages and forms using a slower connection too. Large images and unnecessary page elements can make a site harder to use.

7. Review the website before launch

Do a final review before sharing the website with customers. Use this checklist:

  • Business name, services and contact details are accurate.
  • Every navigation link and important button opens the correct destination.
  • Contact forms submit successfully and enquiries reach the intended recipient.
  • Spelling, page titles and descriptions have been checked.
  • Images display correctly and are permitted for use.
  • Pages work on mobile screens and use a secure HTTPS connection.
  • Privacy and other relevant website information is easy to find.

After launch, review customer questions and update pages when services, prices, contact details or business hours change. A website should be maintained as the business changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pages does a small business website need?

There is no fixed number. Start with the pages visitors need to understand the business, its services and how to make contact. Add more pages when they provide useful information.

Should a business website work on mobile phones?

Yes. A mobile-friendly website makes information easier to read and important actions easier to use on smaller screens. Check the layout and forms on a real phone before launch.

What should a business homepage contain?

It should clearly explain what the business offers, who it serves, the main services or products and the next step a visitor can take. Contact information should be easy to find.

Should I add a portfolio to my business website?

Add a portfolio when examples of previous work help customers understand the service. Use genuine work, obtain permission where needed and describe each example accurately. If you do not have examples to show, do not create fictional projects.
